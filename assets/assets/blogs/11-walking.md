# Walking

I've been walking since childhood. It's never been about performance or hitting some arbitrary step count. It's always been simpler than that — just moving through space and thinking.

## Before Tracking

For years, walking was just something I did. Walk to school. Walk to the market. Walk to clear my head. No app, no data, no validation. Just the rhythm of one foot in front of the other.

The best walks happened when I had something on my mind or when I needed to escape for a bit. No phone distractions, no destination pressure. Just the city or the neighbourhood, whatever was around.

I never thought about how far I went or how long it took. That wasn't the point.

## Strava

Recently I started tracking walks on Strava. Not because I needed to prove anything, but because I was curious about where these walks actually took me.

Strava shows you the route. The elevation. The pace. Sometimes you notice patterns you weren't aware of — favourite paths that loop back, neighbourhoods you drift towards without planning to, time of day preferences.

The data isn't the walk. It's just a trace of it. But seeing those traces mapped out is interesting. It's like looking at your own footprints from above.

## What Changed

The tracking doesn't make the walk better or worse. If anything, I'm more aware now. I notice when I'm rushing versus when I'm actually present. I notice which routes feel good and which ones I do on autopilot.

Some of my best thinking still happens on unremarkable walks. A 30-minute loop through the same streets. Nothing special about the route. But that consistency is probably the point.

Strava gamifies it a bit with segments and leaderboards, but I ignore most of that. The only number that matters is whether I did it or not.

## Still Just Walking

I'm not training for anything. I'm not trying to hit some weekly mileage. I'm doing what I've always done — walking because it feels right to walk.

Strava just lets me remember where I've been.
